CPLR § 2214 — Notice of Motion
Deadlines
Weekend / holiday adjustment — These periods are counted backward from the return date and require service at least a set number of days before it. When the computed day is a Saturday, Sunday or court holiday, the deadline shown has been moved back to the preceding court day, so the full notice period is preserved. Moving it forward to the next business day would leave fewer days than CPLR 2214(b) requires. Gen. Construction Law § 25-a's next-business-day extension is not applied here. Whether § 25-a can extend a backward-counted motion period is not settled, and the earlier date is the safe one.
CPLR § 2214(b) — Time for service of notice and affidavits. A notice of motion and supporting affidavits shall be served at least eight days before the time at which the motion is noticed to be heard. Answering affidavits shall be served at least two days before such time. Answering affidavits and any notice of cross-motion, with supporting papers, if any, shall be served at least seven days before such time if a notice of motion served at least sixteen days before such time so demands; whereupon any reply or responding affidavits shall be served at least one day before such time.
Assumes personal delivery or e-filing (NYSCEF). These dates do not add time for service by mail or overnight delivery. Under CPLR 2103(b)(2), mailing adds 5 days within New York, or 6 days from outside the state but within the U.S.; under CPLR 2103(b)(6), overnight delivery adds one business day. If papers will be served by mail or overnight courier, serve them earlier by that many days.

Not legal advice. This calculator is a convenience tool. It applies CPLR 2214(b) and New York court holidays (federal holidays plus Lincoln's Birthday and Election Day) as programmed, and it does not account for court- or part-specific rules, scheduling orders, adjournments, or stipulations. Always verify deadlines independently against the CPLR, any applicable court rules and orders, and the court's official calendar.
